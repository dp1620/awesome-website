Are AI Features Really Needed?

How Postman got enshittified and why AI products aren't making any value.

Saturday, 15 March 2026

I've been using Postman since it was a Chrome extension. It was simple, fast, and did exactly one thing well: let you test APIs. You'd fire off a request, see the response, save it to a collection, and move on with your life. It was the kind of tool you didn't have to think about. Then somewhere along the way, Postman decided it needed to be a platform. And then it decided it needed AI.

Now every time I open Postman, I'm greeted with AI-powered suggestions I didn't ask for, "smart" features that get in the way of the thing I actually came here to do, and an interface that's gotten so bloated it takes noticeably longer to load than it did three years ago. They added an AI bot called Postbot that can "generate tests" and "explain APIs" — features that sound impressive in a press release but add near-zero value in practice. I already know my API. I wrote it. I don't need an LLM to explain my own endpoints back to me. What I need is a fast, reliable tool that sends HTTP requests. That's it.

Postman isn't alone in this. Every product seems to be racing to slap "AI-powered" on their feature list, regardless of whether it actually helps users. Notion added AI. Figma added AI. Even my terminal wants to autocomplete with AI now. The pattern is always the same: take a tool that works, add a feature nobody asked for, charge more for it, and make the original experience worse in the process. This is textbook enshittification — the product gets worse for users while the company chases a narrative that investors want to hear.

The honest truth is that most of these AI features are solutions looking for problems. They exist because "we added AI" is a growth story, not because anyone sat down with users and heard them say "I wish this had a chatbot." The best tools are the ones that stay out of your way. curl hasn't added AI. grep hasn't added AI. They're still just as useful as they were twenty years ago. Maybe there's a lesson in that.
