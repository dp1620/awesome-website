Things I Learned This Week

March 15, 2026

Every week I stumble into a few things that make me stop and think. This week it was a paper about how most benchmarks are measuring the wrong thing, a conversation about why simple tools outlast complex ones, and the realization that I've been overthinking my side project for months when the MVP was staring me in the face the whole time.

The benchmarks paper was particularly interesting. The authors argued that we optimize for what we measure, and since most benchmarks test narrow, well-defined tasks, we end up building systems that are great at narrow, well-defined tasks but terrible at the messy, ambiguous work that actually matters. This feels true for software engineering in general — we measure lines of code, test coverage, sprint velocity, but the things that actually make software good are much harder to quantify.

As for the side project: I deleted about 2,000 lines of code this week and replaced them with about 200. It does the same thing. I should have started there.
